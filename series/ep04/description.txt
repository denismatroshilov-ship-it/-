You see the first line of the notification, and your thumb moves before you have finished reading it.

Nothing about your money changed in that second. Only how much of it you know.

In this Mind & Money episode we look at why people avoid their own financial information — the ostrich effect, information avoidance, the estimate that anxiety builds in place of the real number, and why the way out is usually not more discipline but a cheaper way to look.

In this video:
• Why avoiding your accounts is often active, not lazy
• The ostrich effect and where it was first observed
• Why not knowing can feel like not losing
• How the imagined number drifts away from the real one
• Separating looking from fixing
• Building a check small enough to survive a bad week
